# NLightning.Application — agent guide

Orchestration layer. It sits between the peer/transport layer (Infrastructure) and the domain plus crypto (Domain, Infrastructure.Bitcoin), and holds:
- PeerManager: the peer table, connect/accept, routing channel messages.
- ChannelManager: dispatches channel messages and reacts to blockchain events.
- The BOLT 2 v1 channel-open handlers and the BOLT 2 normal-operation receive handlers (update_add/fulfill/fail/fail_malformed_htlc, commitment_signed, revoke_and_ack, update_fee; BOLT2 plan N6-T1).
- MessageFactory: builds outbound protocol messages (Infrastructure's `PeerCommunicationService` still constructs error/warning messages directly).
- The send side (BOLT2 plan N6-T2): `IChannelOperations` (`ChannelOperationsService`), the commit scheduler and the `LocalOnlyHtlcSwitch`, which fails every incoming HTLC back once it is locked in (the default until `AddHtlcSwitchServices()` replaces it).
- The HTLC switch (ABCD W2-B): `Payments/Switch/HtlcSwitch` receives (final hop, invoices), forwards, propagates fulfills and failures upstream and replays after a restart. See "Payments" below.
- channel_reestablish (BOLT2 plan N7, ABCD W2-A): `Channels/Reestablish/` and `ChannelReestablishMessageHandler` (see below).
- Cooperative close (BOLT2 plan N10, legacy `closing_signed`): `Channels/Close/` and the `shutdown`/`closing_signed` handlers (see "Cooperative close" below).

- Channel safety (BOLT2 plan N9-T2/T4, ABCD W3): `Channels/Safety/` (HTLC deadline monitor, fail-the-channel broadcast). See "Channel safety" below.

- BOLT 5 wiring (ABCD W5): `Onchain/` (funding-spend watcher, resolution executor; see "BOLT 5 wiring" below) and the resolvers in `Onchain/Resolvers/`.

- `option_simple_close` (BOLT2 plan N11): `Channels/Close/Simple/` and `Channels/Close/Handlers/` (see "option_simple_close" below).

- BOLT 5 mempool reaction (plan O8, NL-098): `Onchain/Mempool/` (see "BOLT 5 mempool reaction" below).

- Chain-processing halt gate (NL-216): see "Chain-processing halt" below.

- BOLT 5 anchor CPFP (plan O7-T2): `Onchain/Anchors/` (see "BOLT 5 anchor CPFP" below).

Not implemented yet: enabling `option_anchors` (O7-T4; NL-094). O7-T1 wallet signing is in Infrastructure.Bitcoin; O7-T3 (anchors HTLC transactions with wallet fee inputs, their RBF and conflict rebuild, penalty isolation) is in `Onchain/Resolvers/`.

The payment core (ABCD W1-B) lives in `Payments/`: incoming onion processing, final hop, forwarding policy, route and onion building for our payments, and invoices. `Payments/Switch/` (W2-B) is the switch that calls it; the send path (`Payments/Send/PaymentService`, ABCD W2-C) uses it and offers our HTLCs through `IChannelOperations`. See "Payments" below.

## Layout
- `DependencyInjection.cs`: `AddApplicationServices()`. Registers the Domain services that have no DI of their own (IChannelOpenValidator, IChannelFactory, ICommitmentTransactionModelFactory, IFundingTransactionModelFactory; they need the host's IFeeService and AddBitcoinInfrastructure's ILightningSigner) and IChannelLockProvider, `ChannelManager` (built by a factory lambda, then exposed as both `IChannelManager` and `IChannelMessagePublisher`: one instance), IMessageFactory and IPeerManager as singletons, plus `AddCommitmentEngineServices()`, `AddChannelStateTransitionServices()` and `AddChannelOperationsServices()` (all in `Channels/Services/CommitmentEngineServiceCollectionExtensions.cs`), `Channels/Close/CloseServiceCollectionExtensions.AddChannelCloseServices()`, `Gossip.AddGossipServices()` and `Payments.AddPaymentsServices()`. A reflection scan registers every `IChannelMessageHandler<>` as Scoped. `FundingConfirmedMessageHandler` is registered as a concrete Scoped type. It is called from `src/NLightning.Daemon/Extensions/NodeServiceExtensions.cs`.
- `Channels/Handlers/Interfaces/IChannelMessageHandler.cs`: `Task<IReadOnlyList<IChannelMessage>> HandleAsync(msg, ChannelState currentState, FeatureOptions negotiatedFeatures, CompactPubKey peerPubKey)`. The returned messages are sent back to the same peer in list order (e.g. revoke_and_ack before commitment_signed); return `[]` for none.
- `Channels/Services/ChannelLockProvider.cs`: `IChannelLockProvider` (Domain) singleton, one non-reentrant `SemaphoreSlim` per channel id, dropped when unused. `AcquireAsync` / blocking `Acquire` return an `IDisposable`.
- `Channels/Handlers/`:
  - `OpenChannel1MessageHandler`: we are the non-initiator. Receives open_channel, replies accept_channel with **our** values (`ChannelParams.Local`, never the opener's; NL-194) and the opener's `channel_type` echoed byte for byte (BOLT 2, NL-218).
  - `AcceptChannel1MessageHandler`: we are the initiator. Receives accept_channel, fails it if `channel_type` differs from the one we sent (`ChannelParams.ToChannelType()`) or a reserve is below the other side's dust limit, stores the peer's values with `ChannelModel.UpdateRemoteParams` (our own, incl. `to_self_delay`, never change), builds the funding tx, replies funding_created. The funding is sized with a P2WPKH probe change address first; a change address is reserved from the wallet (`GetUnusedAddressAsync`, NL-280) only when the funding has a change output, and replaces the probe. The channel is not persisted until funding_signed (BOLT 2: a funder that has not broadcast SHOULD NOT remember the channel).
  - `FundingCreatedMessageHandler`: non-initiator. Replies funding_signed, persists the channel and watches the funding tx.
  - `FundingSignedMessageHandler`: initiator. Signs the funding tx, then saves in **one** save the channel as V1FundingSigned, the funding watch, the signed funding tx as a pending `BroadcastTransactionModel` (purpose `Funding`) and the funding output's `WatchedOutpointModel` (BOLT 5 plan O0-T1/T2), tracks both watches in the monitor and publishes through `IChainBroadcaster.PublishAsync` (the monitor); a refused publish is only logged, because the monitor sends every stored pending tx again after each block and at startup (NL-258). `IChannelMemoryRepository.UpdateChannel` (the open subscription's V1FundingSigned event) is raised only after the publish. Sends no reply. If anything before that save fails (bad signature, rebuilt funding mismatch, signing), the wallet outputs locked to the channel are released (`ReturnUtxosNotSpentOnChannel`) before the exception goes on (NL-259); at the next start `ChannelManager` persists the V1FundingCreated channel Stale (no funding watch), abandons any pending `Funding`/`Unspecified` broadcast row of it in that same save and releases its UTXO locks. A funding bitcoind refuses for good is abandoned by the chain monitor (NL-294, `Infrastructure.Bitcoin/Wallet/BroadcastRefusalRules`), which releases the locks too; the channel itself stays V1FundingSigned in memory (forgetting it is a follow-up).
  - `ChannelReadyMessageHandler`: on the **first** channel_ready (the remote key set still at the first index) it builds the channel's first commitment snapshot (`ChannelStateTransitionService.CreateInitialCommitments`: balances, feerate, both commitment numbers, the peer's signature of our commitment 0, the peer's current point from the key set and the next point from the message) before the key set's point is overwritten (NL-232), and saves it with `IChannelStateDbRepository.InitializeAsync` in the same save as the channel; the model gets it only after the save. If the snapshot can't be built it logs and the channel works without HTLCs. Channels that received channel_ready before this existed have lost the peer's commitment-0 point and never get a snapshot (logged at startup; their HTLC messages get a warning).
  - Normal operation (N6-T1), all through the scoped `Services/ChannelStateTransitionService`: `UpdateAddHtlcMessageHandler`, `UpdateFulfillHtlcMessageHandler`, `UpdateFailHtlcMessageHandler`, `UpdateFailMalformedHtlcMessageHandler` (a failure_code without BADONION: warning + close, checked first), `UpdateFeeMessageHandler` (feerate bounds `MinAcceptableFeeratePerKw` 253 / `MaxAcceptableFeeratePerKw` 250,000), `CommitmentSignedMessageHandler` (replies revoke_and_ack, then our commitment_signed when changes are pending for the peer) and `RevokeAndAckMessageHandler` (inserts the secret into a fresh copy of the peer's shachain loaded from `IRemoteShachainDbRepository`, saves it with the transition as `ChannelStateExtras.RemoteShachain`, then signs if changes are pending). Each: guard (`GetUpdatableChannel`), engine call, `CommitAsync` (`ApplyAsync` + one `SaveChangesAsync`, then `ChannelModel.UpdateCommitments` + memory update, then the transition's events are queued), then the replies. Engine violations (`CommitmentViolationException`) become `ChannelWarningException { CloseConnection = true }` (BOLT 2's warning-and-close option; the engine state is unchanged and reestablish recovers), except `MustFailChannel` ones (bad revocation secret, B2-RAA-R01) which become `ChannelFailedException`.
  - `FundingConfirmedMessageHandler`: not an IChannelMessageHandler. It is triggered by a funding confirmation and emits channel_ready through its `OnMessageReady` event.
- `Channels/Services/CommitmentSigningService.cs` (BOLT2 plan N3-T5): a concrete service (no interface) with `SignRemoteCommitment(channel, CommitmentTxSpec, remoteNumber, remotePoint)` and `VerifyLocalCommitment(channel, CommitmentTxSpec, localNumber, sig, htlcSigs)` (throws `SignerException`), both returning `CommitmentTxSignatures(txid, sig, htlcSigs)`. It runs factory (spec-driven overload) -> `BuildWithOutputMap` -> `HtlcTransactionModelFactory` -> `IHtlcTransactionBuilder.Build` -> `ILightningSigner` (`SignChannelTransaction` + `SignRemoteHtlcTransactions`, or `ValidateSignature` + `ValidateLocalHtlcSignatures`), so HTLC signatures are always in commitment output order. Vectors: `test/NLightning.Integration.Tests/BOLT3/CommitmentSigningServiceVectorTests` (Appendix C/F: as node B the signatures equal `remote_signature`/`remote_htlc_signature`; as node A the vector signatures verify).
- Engine ports (NL-230), same folder: `EngineCommitmentSignerPort : ICommitmentSigner` and `EngineCommitmentVerifierPort : ICommitmentVerifier` (the Domain engine ports in `Channels/Commitments/Interfaces`) look the channel up in `IChannelMemoryRepository` by id (static data only), adapt the engine's `CommitmentSpec` with `CommitmentTxSpec.FromCommitmentSpec`, and call `CommitmentSigningService`; they reject a spec of the wrong holder (`ArgumentException`) and an unknown channel (`InvalidOperationException`). The verifier maps `SignerException` to `false` (the engine then raises B2-CS-R01) and logs it; other exceptions propagate. `EngineRevocationVerifierPort : IRevocationVerifier` delegates to `IPerCommitmentSecretVerifier.Verify`. All four are singletons registered by `CommitmentEngineServiceCollectionExtensions.AddCommitmentEngineServices()` (called from `AddApplicationServices`). Proof: `test/NLightning.Application.Tests/Channels/Services/EngineCommitmentPortsTwoNodeTests` drives two engines with real `LocalLightningSigner`s built from the production DI (`RealSigningCommitmentPair`) through add/CS/RAA/fulfill/fail/update_fee, with and without anchors, and checks that the txid each side signed equals the txid the other verified; tampered commitment/HTLC signatures fail B2-CS-R01 and a wrong secret B2-RAA-R01. The same harness records each node's domain events (`RealSigningNode.Events`, N4-T4) and checks lock-in once on revoke_and_ack and fail/settle only when irrevocable. The N6-T1 receive handlers drive the engine through `ChannelStateTransitionService`. `ChannelManager` hands the `IHtlcSwitch` (Domain, `Channels/Interfaces`; `LocalOnlyHtlcSwitch` by default) the events of each persisted transition, and replays `ChannelDomainEvents.DerivePending` at startup.
- `Channels/Services/ChannelStateTransitionService.cs` (scoped, N6-T1): `GetUpdatableChannel(id, state, name)` is the state guard (HTLCs disabled by `NodeOptions.HtlcsEnabled` → the old "not supported yet" warning; channel not in memory or without a snapshot → warning; `Failed` → `error` again; not `Open` → warning + close, B2-NO-02); `CommitAsync(channel, result, extras)` persists one transition (D3/I1/I2); `CreateRevokeAndAck` calls `ILightningSigner.AdvanceLocalCommitment` then `RevealPerCommitmentSecret` (only after the save, I3); `SignIfPendingAsync` signs the peer's next commitment when `ChannelCommitments.CanSendCommit` (never after data loss) and saves it with `ChannelStateExtras.SentCommitDiff` (our pending updates rebuilt from the snapshot — adds by id, removals by id, fee — then the commitment_signed, in `SentCommitDiffCodec` format: u16 big-endian length + serialized message each) and `LastSent`; `ToWireMessage` maps engine outbound messages to wire messages (`OutboundStartBatch` → `start_batch`, a member's `FundingTxId` else the channel's funding txid); `SignPendingAsync` is the list form of the signature (one `commitment_signed`, or with pending splices `start_batch` + one per active funding, the whole batch in the sent diff) and `SignIfPendingAsync` its single-message form, which with a pending splice signs nothing and schedules the `ICommitScheduler` (optional constructor argument) instead; `ReceiveCommitmentSignedBatchAsync(channel, CommitmentSignedBatch)` (needs the optional `ICommitmentVerifier` argument) is the batch receive (SP-OP-05/06/07: engine checks, one save, then the one revoke_and_ack's secret, then our own signature via `SignPendingAsync`), called by `ChannelManager.HandleCommitmentSignedBatchAsync` under one acquisition of the channel lock with the gates of a single `commitment_signed`, and refused (warning + close) unless `option_splice` (experimental) was negotiated, because the per-funding signatures, pending fundings and revoked fundings are not persisted before lane SP1-C (SP1-C-T4; SP-I3 holds only then); `ChannelManager.Publish` drops a `start_batch` with the members it announces when the channel is not reestablished on the current connection (B2-RE-07); the close coordinator refuses `closechannel` and defers our `shutdown` while `ChannelCommitments.PendingFundings` is not empty (BOLT 2: no shutdown before the splice locks) and signs pending updates with the list form `SignPendingAsync`; `LoadRemoteShachainAsync` gives a fresh store loaded from the database. `ChannelDomainEventQueue` (scoped) collects the events of the persisted transitions. `SecretStorageServiceFactory` (`ISecretStorageServiceFactory`, `TryAddSingleton`) makes `SecretStorageService` instances. All registered by `AddChannelStateTransitionServices()` (in `CommitmentEngineServiceCollectionExtensions`, called from `AddApplicationServices`).
- Channels without a snapshot (channel_ready handled before ABCD wave 1, NL-246) cannot be given one: the first snapshot needs the peer's per-commitment point of its current commitment 0, which that channel_ready replaced with the second point, and nothing we store yields it (we keep no remote commitment 0 transaction, and a signature does not reveal the key). `channel_reestablish` is no way back: BOLT 2 now says the receiver MUST ignore `my_current_per_commitment_point` (the sender only has to send a valid point). Such a channel keeps working without HTLCs (HTLC messages get a warning); close and reopen it once N10 exists, and never build a snapshot from an unverified point.
- Send side (N6-T2), `Channels/Interfaces/` and `Channels/Services/`:
  - `ChannelOperationsService : IChannelOperations` (singleton): Offer/Fulfill/Fail/FailMalformed/UpdateFee each take the channel's lock, check the preconditions (`NodeOptions.HtlcsEnabled`, `Open`, not `Failed`, no data loss, a snapshot, and for **every** operation the channel's link is up: `IPeerLivenessProbe.IsAliveAsync(channelId, peer)`), run the engine (its sender rules throw `CommitmentRefusedException`), persist with `ChannelStateTransitionService.CommitAsync` (one save; its optional `stageWithTransition` callback stages more writes on the same unit of work before that save: the offer's `HtlcOrigin`, and the caller's writes of the `FulfillHtlcAsync` overload with `stageWithFulfill`), publish the wire message through `IChannelMessagePublisher` under the lock, then `ICommitScheduler.Schedule` after the lock. Nothing is persisted or sent on a refusal; an unknown channel throws `KeyNotFoundException`. A removal or fee update for a peer that is away (or on a connection the channel was not opened/reestablished on) is refused like an offer: `PeerManager` drops messages for a peer that is not connected and nothing retransmits before N7, so a persisted-but-unsent update would later be covered by a `commitment_signed` the peer can't verify. The HTLC stays locked in and the switch acts again on the replay (startup, and with `AddHtlcSwitchServices` every `MarkLinkUp`, see the HTLC switch section; N7 must call `MarkLinkUp` after the reestablish). Left for N7: an update enqueued just as its connection closes is lost with that connection (the link is then down for good, so nothing signs over it; N7's reestablish must forget/retransmit it). `RecordOnionSecretAsync` stores the incoming HTLC's Sphinx secret (`IChannelStateDbRepository.SetOnionSharedSecretAsync`, one save). The `HtlcOrigin` of an offer is validated and staged with `IChannelStateDbRepository.SetHtlcOriginAsync` in the add's save (NL-250), so a restart routes the outgoing HTLC's resolution back to its circuit or payment. "channel_reestablish processed" is approximated by the pinned link (N7 must call `MarkLinkUp`).
  - `CommitScheduler : ICommitScheduler` (singleton, `CommitSchedulerOptions.Debounce`, default 10 ms): one waiting round per channel, run on the thread pool without the caller's execution context (so its message goes to the peer's current connection); `SignNowAsync` asks `IPeerLivenessProbe` (ping-before-commit) before the lock and again under it (a reconnection meanwhile must not get a commitment_signed before channel_reestablish), then under the lock `ChannelStateTransitionService.SignPendingAsync` (never while `RemoteNextCommit` exists, D7; persisted with the diff; a batch while splices are pending) and publishes every message in one `Publish`. `WhenIdleAsync` for tests. A failed signature is logged; the changes stay pending. The receive handlers still sign on their own after commitment_signed/revoke_and_ack.
  - `IPeerLivenessProbe` → `ConnectedPeerLivenessProbe` (singleton): channel-scoped. `MarkLinkUp(channelId, peer)` pins the peer's current connection (the `PeerModel` instance `IPeerManager.GetPeer` returns; one per connection); `IsAliveAsync(channelId, peer)` is true only while `GetPeer` still returns that same instance, so after a reconnection the channel is down until marked again. `ChannelManager` marks a channel when it turns `Open` (channel_ready handled, or our funding confirmation); a channel loaded at startup or reconnected is marked after its channel_reestablish (N7), and the production registration gates the probe with the `ReestablishTracker` (`ReestablishGatedLivenessProbe`). Without a peer manager (in-process tests) a marked channel is up. The peer manager is resolved lazily. The BOLT 2 ping before `commitment_signed` is not the probe's job: `CommitScheduler.SignNowAsync` asks `IPingBeforeCommit` (`PingBeforeCommit`, singleton, `TryAdd`ed by `AddChannelOperationsServices`) after the cheap link check and before taking the lock; it pings through `IPeerService.PingAsync` when `IPeerService.LastMessageReceivedAt` is older than `CommitSchedulerOptions.PingWhenQuietFor` (30 s) and waits `PongTimeout` (10 s). No pong: nothing is signed and the connection is closed (BOLT 1 MAY), so the next connection's reestablish signs the pending changes; without a peer manager (in-process harnesses) every peer counts as responsive (NL-251).
  - `IChannelMessagePublisher`: implemented by `ChannelManager.Publish` (raises `OnResponseMessageReady`); call it under the channel's lock. It drops normal-operation messages (128-135) of a channel that is not reestablished on the peer's current connection (the connection can be replaced during the caller's save; the reestablish retransmits what was persisted), checked and raised under the same gate as `OnPeerConnectionChanged`, which `PeerManager` calls before it swaps the session, so nothing precedes our channel_reestablish on a new connection (B2-RE-07).
  - `Channels/Switch/LocalOnlyHtlcSwitch : IHtlcSwitch` (`TryAddSingleton`; replace it with `services.Replace(...)`): on `IncomingHtlcLockedIn` it skips an HTLC that is no longer in `RcvdAddAckRevocation` (idempotent replay), fails a blinded HTLC with `invalid_onion_blinding` (malformed), peels with `ISphinxService.PeelAsLocalNode(packet, payment_hash)` (BADONION → `update_fail_malformed_htlc` with the exception's sha256_of_onion; `invalid_onion_payload` with a secret → encrypted), records the shared secret, then fails with `incorrect_or_unknown_payment_details(htlc_msat, IBlockchainMonitor.LastProcessedBlockHeight)` when final and the height is known, or `temporary_node_failure` when it would forward or no block was processed yet (height 0 would mislead the payer); no BOLT 4 final-node checks yet (`final_incorrect_cltv_expiry`/`final_incorrect_htlc_amount`, W2-B), encrypted by `IFailureOnionService.CreateErrorPacket`. No replay-cache entry. Outgoing fulfilled/failed are logged; `OutgoingHtlcSettled` prunes the archived row (`PruneSettledHtlcsAsync`, one save, NL-243). A `CommitmentRefusedException`/`KeyNotFoundException` is logged, not thrown.
- `Channels/Managers/ChannelManager.cs`: the `switch (message.Type)` in `DispatchChannelMessageAsync`. `HandleChannelMessageAsync` creates the message scope **before** taking the lock; after the lock is released it drains the scope's `ChannelDomainEventQueue` into the registered `IHtlcSwitch` (a failing switch is logged, the events are re-derivable), also when the handler threw after a persisted transition. A `ChannelFailedException` from a handler is persisted first (under the lock): `ChannelState.Failed` and the serialized `error` (`ChannelModel.MarkErrorSent`) through `ChannelDbRepository.UpdateAsync` + save (skipped when the channel is already Failed with a stored error), then rethrown (PeerManager sends the error and keeps the connection). funding_created runs under its temporary id's lock **and** the real channel id's lock (computed with `IChannelIdFactory`, temporary first; NL-235): the only place two locks are held. `RegisterExistingChannelAsync` first resumes the stored state (N7-T5): Closed/Stale and pre-funding states (None, V1Opening, V2Opening) are not registered; a funder's `V1FundingCreated` (only from builds before BOLT 5 plan O0, which saved the channel before the publish and V1FundingSigned after it; the handler now saves V1FundingSigned with the funding tx in one save) becomes V1FundingSigned when a watched transaction with its funding txid and channel id is stored (the watch is saved before the publish, so the tx may be out) and is persisted Stale and not registered otherwise (BOLT 2: a funder that has not broadcast SHOULD NOT remember; NL-048 wontfix, its locked UTXOs are not released); V1FundingSigned, ReadyFor*, ShuttingDown, Negotiating, Closing (see "Cooperative close") and Failed are registered as they are. It also keeps `Failed` channels in memory, and for a channel with a snapshot runs `ChannelCommitments.RevertUncommitted` and persists it when it changed something (before any reestablish); for an `Open` channel it then derives the pending events (`ChannelDomainEvents.DerivePending` over the snapshot and the unpruned settled rows from `IChannelStateDbRepository.LoadAsync`; a failure is logged and skipped) and hands them to the switch **after** releasing the lock (I8). At startup no channel's link is up, so a replayed fail is refused (logged by the switch) and the HTLC stays locked in until the replay after N7's reestablish. `HandleChannelMessageAsync` (a channel that was not `Open` before the message and is after it) and the funding confirmation (after `FundingConfirmedMessageHandler`) call `IPeerLivenessProbe.MarkLinkUp` under the lock. Also handles blockchain events: `HandleNewBlockDetected` (ForgetStaleChannels, ConfirmUnconfirmedChannels) and `HandleFundingConfirmationAsync`.
- `Node/Managers/PeerManager.cs`: `ConcurrentDictionary<CompactPubKey, PeerSession>`; a session is one connection: the `PeerModel`, a bounded inbound queue (1024, the transport read loop waits when full) drained by one loop, a close token, and the connection's `PeerOutbox`. A connection becomes a session only after `IPeerService.WaitForInitAsync` (both inits exchanged; its preferred address and features come from that init): `ConnectToPeerAsync` returns only then and throws `ConnectionException` (after disposing the peer service) if the connection closes first, and an inbound connection is handled on its own task (`HandleNewPeerConnectedAsync`) and dropped with an info log in that case. So neither end ever keeps a connection the other cannot use (NL-240). `StopAsync` sets `_stopping` under the session lock and cancels the pending init waits (`WaitForInitAsync` gets a stopping token; the connection is disconnected and disposed); `TryInstallSession` refuses under the same lock once stopping (`InstallResult.Stopping`), so every installed session is in the disconnect pass, and `StopAsync` also waits (5 s timeout) for the inbound setups still running (`_inboundSetups`, registered before any of the setup runs), so no peer is saved to the database after it returns. Sessions subscribe to the peer service right after that (before any DB work; `PeerService` replays channel messages and a disconnection that happened before), and only then are installed. Closing a session cancels its loop (queued messages are dropped, the one being handled finishes); the next session of the same peer starts its loop only after the previous loop ended, and `StopAsync` waits for all loops. Replies raised while a loop handles a message go to that loop's session (an `AsyncLocal`), so they are dropped rather than sent on a newer connection. A new connection replaces the current one, except within `SimultaneousConnectWindow` (5 s) of an opposite-direction connection, where the connection initiated by the lower pubkey is kept (LND's `shouldDropLocalConnection`). A runtime drop of a peer with active channels in `IChannelMemoryRepository` starts the backoff reconnect loop unless the disconnect was ours (`DisconnectPeer`, `StopAsync`, replacement). It subscribes to `IChannelManager.OnResponseMessageReady` in its constructor and only enqueues there (never throws). Maps exceptions (through the outbox, so they follow the replies queued before them), always scoped to the message's channel id (never an all-zero `error`, BOLT 1): ChannelFailedException sends `error` for the channel and keeps the connection (N7), any other ChannelErrorException sends `error` for the channel and disconnects, ChannelWarningException sends a `warning` and stays connected (or disconnects after it when `CloseConnection` is set), any other exception (our bug) sends a channel `warning` and disconnects. `start_batch` (127) never reaches the channel manager as a message: the inbound loop groups it (SP1-A-T3, BOLT 2 "Batching channel messages", SP-OP-04): `batch_size` <= 1 is ignored with a (non-closing) channel `warning`; > 20 (`MaxBatchSize`) is a `warning` and close; a missing or non-132 `message_type` is ignored and the following messages are handled one by one; otherwise the next `batch_size` messages are collected (per connection, dropped with it) and handed to `IChannelManager.HandleCommitmentSignedBatchAsync` in one call as a `CommitmentSignedBatch`, its failures mapped like a single message's; a grouped message for another channel, or one that is not a `commitment_signed`, is a `warning` and close scoped to the batch's channel. Tests: `test/NLightning.Application.Tests/Node/Managers/PeerManagerBatchTests.cs`.
- `PeerManager.StartAsync` (NL-201): awaits `IChannelManager.RegisterExistingChannelAsync` (takes the channel lock; memory, plus the signer only when no `IChannelSigningInfoSource` is registered: the production signer loads channels from the database itself, NL-343; a source-less signer (in-process tests) is also registered again at the funding confirmation so it knows the real SCID) for every non-Closed/Stale channel of every stored peer before connecting to any of them; a failing channel is logged and skipped. - Wiring: `ChannelManager` dispatches `shutdown`/`closing_signed` (gated like normal-operation messages until the channel is reestablished) and calls `AdvanceAsync` after every handled message of a ShuttingDown/Negotiating channel (a failure there is logged, never sent to the peer). Never on a channel closing on chain (`Failed`/`OnchainResolving`): no failure can be sent there, so a forward is left to time out and a final-hop HTLC of ours is accepted and claimed on chain (NL-316) like any other. ## option_simple_close (BOLT2 plan N11: N11-T1, N11-T2; `Channels/Close/Simple/`, `Channels/Close/Handlers/`)
- Negotiated per connection: `ClosingNegotiationRegistry.Entry.SimpleClose` is set from the negotiated features of the peer's `shutdown` (`ChannelCloseCoordinator.ReceiveShutdownAsync`), `closing_complete` or `closing_sig`, and reset by a new connection. With it, `AdvanceAsync` never opens a legacy negotiation: once Negotiating (both shutdowns, `IsCleared`) and both shutdowns went over the current connection, `SimpleCloseCoordinator.ProposeIfDueAsync` sends our own `closing_complete` once per connection (both sides do, funder or not); a peer's `closing_signed` gets a `warning` (message ignored). Without it the legacy close is unchanged (the fallback, and the default: `FeatureOptions.OptionSimpleClose` is No).
- `Simple/SimpleClosingTerms` (pure: closer/closee balances in msat, both scripts, `fee_satoshis`; `CloserAmountSat` = balance rounded down minus the fee, 0 for OP_RETURN; `CloseeAmountSat`; per-script dust via `ShutdownScriptValidator.GetDustThresholdSat`, OP_RETURN never dust; `Build(ClosingSigKind)`) and `Simple/SimpleCloseRules` (pure: `SelectCloserKinds` = B2-SC-C01/C02/C06/C07 (lesser side: `closee_output_only` when its own output is dust, else `closer_and_closee_outputs`; otherwise `closer_output_only` alone when the closee is dust, else both 1 and 3; refusals carry the requirement id), `SelectCloseeKind` = B2-SC-E06, `ChooseFee` = feerate (floor 253) x `ClosingFeeCalculator.EstimateWeight` of both outputs, capped at our balance and, when we are not the lesser side, so our own output stays at its dust threshold; null (no proposal) below the floor fee). We never use an OP_RETURN script or the "uneconomical" MAY rows.

A Closing channel still signs the peer's new `closing_complete` and bumps ours. Every protocol failure is `warning` + close (never fail the channel: BOLT 2 allows either, and failing would broadcast our commitment while a mutual close is possible). No timeout for a missing `closing_sig` yet (BOLT 2 SHOULD close the connection): the only disconnect API, `IPeerManager.DisconnectPeer`, also suppresses our reconnect loop, so it waits for a `PeerManager` disconnect that keeps reconnecting; until then an unanswered proposal blocks RBF (B2-SC-C09) until the next connection.
- Whichever transaction confirms closes the channel: `ChannelManager.IsMutualCloseOf` accepts the simple-close shape (sequence 0xFFFFFFFD, any lock time; one or two outputs, at most one of them not to our script) as well as the legacy one (0xFFFFFFFF, lock time 0, every output to one of the two current scripts), so a spend by a transaction other than the stored one becomes the closing transaction (the existing funding-spend path) and reaches Closed at depth. That is safe because the funding output needs our signature and we sign that sequence only on simple closing transactions. Watches of the replaced transactions stay pending forever (no delete API).
- `ChannelCloseCoordinator.ReceiveClosingCompleteAsync`/`ReceiveClosingSigAsync` (called by `Close/Handlers/ClosingCompleteMessageHandler`/`ClosingSigMessageHandler`; the first moves a cleared ShuttingDown channel to Negotiating) and `BumpSimpleCloseAsync`; `ChannelCloseService`: a request with a feerate on a Negotiating/Closing channel with simple close negotiated on this connection sends a new `closing_complete` (RBF) instead of only reporting; one it can't send (a Closing channel without the flag on this connection, or a Negotiating one whose flag was set on an earlier connection, `Entry.SimpleCloseSeen`) throws `InvalidOperationException` instead of being dropped. Txids are logged in display order (NL-275). ## BOLT 5 wiring (ABCD W5, plan O2-T5, O6-T2; `Onchain/`)
Registered by `OnchainServiceCollectionExtensions.AddOnchainServices()` (TryAdd singletons, from `AddApplicationServices` after `AddChannelSafetyServices`); `OnchainOptions` from `Node:Onchain` (`ReasonableDepth` 6 for the resolvers, `IrrevocableDepth` 100). - `OnchainChannelWatcher` (NL-272): under the channel's lock, idempotent (a spend already recorded at the same height and block is `Replayed`, nothing written; the same tx at another height or block (a reorg) is `Replayed` with only the close's `SpentAtHeight`/`BlockHash` updated, so the irrevocable depth counts from the new block; a different recorded spend is overwritten, NL-292). After the lock: `TrackWatchedOutpoint`, the error through `IChannelErrorSender` (B5-GEN-04), critical alerts (B5-GEN-06 unknown, B5-RMT-03 future, B5-GEN-06 for vouts the mapper could not identify (`CommitmentOutputMap.UnmappedVouts`, with amounts; "predates the revocation log" for a revoked number below `GetLogStartAsync`)), `IOnchainResolutionExecutor.CatchUpSpendsAsync(new watches, spend height)` (the monitor keeps processing blocks while the spend is classified: an output spent in the commitment's block or a block processed before the watch was tracked is found by scanning those blocks), then `ResolveChannelAsync(spend height)`. - `OnchainResolutionExecutor`: per channel in `OnchainResolving`, under its lock, one unit of work: a spent output → `Resolved` at the spend height (unless already Irrevocable/Ignored or resolved at that height) and `IOutputResolver.OnOutputSpentAsync`; every `Resolved` output 100 deep → `Irrevocable`; `ResolveAsync(close, outputs, height)` of the first resolver (`GetServices<IOutputResolver>()` from the round's scope) whose `CanResolve(kind)`; when the funding spend and every output are irrevocable (or `Ignored`) → `Closed` + the revocation log deleted, in the same save (Closed is staged on the database's copy from `ChannelDbRepository.GetByIdAsync`; the shared model turns Closed and leaves memory only after the save, so a failed save is retried next round), and the channel is removed from memory. After the save: new watches tracked, then caught up (`CatchUpSpendsAsync`: blocks from the watch's parent height (commitment height, the handled spend's height, our broadcast's `ConfirmedHeight`, for a pending broadcast the monitor's `LastProcessedBlockHeight` read before the round) to bitcoind's tip through `IBitcoinChainService` when registered; a spend found runs like a monitor event and is recorded on the watch with `MarkSpentAsync` in that round's save; NL-311: in the first round of the process, after every channel's resolution (the time-critical part comes first), one block scan (`CatchUpSavedWatchesAsync`) does the same for the saved watch of every `Pending`/`Waiting`/`Broadcast` row of every channel not caught up yet, from its parent height or from the spend the monitor recorded on it, so a crash between a save and `TrackWatchedOutpoint`, or between the monitor's block save and the spend's handling, misses nothing; a scan stopped by a chain read error resumes in the next round at the first block it did not scan (memory only; a restart scans from the parent height again)), new or still pending broadcast rows published (`IChainBroadcaster`), `IChannelDomainEvent`s to the scope's `IHtlcSwitch`, alerts logged. A close kind without a resolver is logged once and never closes (its outputs stay Pending). The resolvers (`Onchain/Resolvers/`, below) are registered right after `AddOnchainServices()` in `AddApplicationServices` (`AddLocalCommitResolutionServices`, `AddRemoteCommitResolutionServices`, `AddRevokedCommitResolver`); exactly one handles each commitment kind (Local; Remote/RemoteNext/Future/Unknown; Revoked), none handles Mutual (`Daemon.Tests` `OnchainClientHandlerTests` checks the composed graph). The daemon binds `LocalCommitResolverOptions`, `RemoteResolutionOptions` and `RevokedCommitResolverOptions` from `Node:Onchain` too.
- Fee bumping (O6-T1, NL-317): `Onchain/Fees/SweepScheduler : ISweepScheduler` (singleton from `AddOnchainServices`, with the shared `SweepFeePolicy` built from `OnchainOptions.FeePolicy` = `Node:Onchain:FeePolicy`, which the resolvers use too) runs in every block round of the executor after the resolver (never on a spend event): each `Pending` broadcast of purpose `Sweep`/`HtlcClaim`/`Penalty` whose `SweepFeePolicy.ShouldBump(FirstBroadcastHeight, tip, deadline)` holds (deadline = the earliest `DeadlineHeight` of the rows it spends; without one, `SweepConfTarget` blocks) is re-signed with the same inputs, sequences, lock time and destination at `SweepFeePolicy.DecideReplacement` (BIP 125 minimum or the estimate for the deadline's target, `FeeEstimates.GetForTargetAsync`, NL-296). Every input must spend one of the channel's rows: the key comes from the row's descriptor (`DelayedToLocal` → delayed key at its point, `PaymentToRemote` → payment basepoint, the peer's HTLC outputs → HTLC key at its point, `Revoked*` → revocation key with the peer's secret of `close.CommitmentNumber` from the shachain), amount and witness script from its `OutputDescriptorData`; only witness item 0 (the signature) changes. The replacement row (`ReplacesTransactionId`, `FirstBroadcastHeight` = tip), the rows moved to it and the old row `Replaced` go into the round's save; the executor publishes after it. It also retires what can no longer confirm (NL-294 partial): a pending one with an input spent on chain by another transaction is `Abandoned`, a pending one no row names that a later row replaces (a split penalty batch) is `Replaced`. A transaction it cannot re-sign or outbid within the cap is kept and logged once. Pre-signed non-anchor HTLC txs and our commitment are never bumped here: anchors HTLC txs are RBF-bumped by `LocalCommitResolver` and the anchors commitment through its CPFP child by `AnchorCpfpService` (NL-387: the class remark is stale).
- Reorgs (O6-T3, NL-292): in every block round the executor first unresolves rows whose watched spend the chain monitor rolled back (`Resolved` with an unspent watch → `Broadcast` when `ResolvingTransactionId` is set (that broadcast made pending again, `MarkPendingAsync`, and published after the round's save, since the resolvers never re-emit a broadcast for a row that names its resolving transaction), else `Pending`; a spend found at another height moves `ResolvedHeight`), then checks that `close.BlockHash` is still bitcoind's block at `SpentAtHeight` (skipped when the hash is empty or there is no `IBitcoinChainService`). Pause and grace are process memory: after a restart the check pauses the channel again and the grace restarts. ## BOLT 5 local commitment resolution (plan O3-T3/T4; `Onchain/Resolvers/`)
- `LocalCommitResolver : IOutputResolver` (Domain port, W5-A; `CanResolve(LocalCommitment)`) resolves **our** commitment on chain. No selection, or any failure, broadcasts nothing and the next block tries again; a shortage (or no provider: the default `UnavailableAnchorFeeInputProvider` logs nothing) is a warning once per output and process, debug afterwards. Signing: `ILightningSigner.SignAnchorInput` (funding key over the anchor script built by the signer, 330 sat only) and `SignWalletTransaction` (must sign only the wallet inputs; P2TR inputs need the anchor prevout, see `IAnchorFeeInputSource`), then every input is script-checked (`Transaction.CreateValidator`) before the row is stored; a signing failure (e.g. `NotImplementedException` before O7-T1) drops the child and releases the fresh reservation (logged once); so does a failed save of a first child (released before the exception ends the round). Row: `AnchorCpfp`, `FeeratePerKw` = the child's own feerate over its signed weight.
- RBF: when `SweepFeePolicy.ShouldBump(child.FirstBroadcastHeight, tip, deadline)` (past the deadline it keeps bumping every `RbfIntervalBlocks`) and (with a deadline, always, as `SweepScheduler` bumps claims; without one, only while) the package pays less than the estimate, `AnchorCpfpPolicy.DecideReplacement` (BIP 125 minimum over the old fee's upper bound `(rate + 1) * weight / 1000 + 1`, or the estimate, capped; none when the minimum is above the cap): same anchor (the conflict), the channel's reserved inputs plus more when needed, the old change script; the new row (`ReplacesTransactionId`, `FirstBroadcastHeight` = tip) and the old row `Replaced` in one save.
- End: commitment `Abandoned` or `Replaced` → pending children `Abandoned` (never rebroadcast) and the reservation released at once (no child can confirm). Commitment `Confirmed` with a pending child: the child may still confirm (it spends a confirmed output), so it stays pending (rebroadcast by the monitor, never bumped) and the reservation is kept (a funding tx taking its inputs would conflict with it under BIP 125 and be refused forever, NL-294) until our anchor is spent in the active chain (`IBitcoinChainService.GetConfirmedUnspentOutputAsync`; every child spends it) and a later round runs at or above the bitcoind tip read when that was seen (the monitor processed the spending block, so a winning child is `Confirmed`), or `ConfirmedCommitmentChildWaitBlocks` (2016) passed since the confirmation; then the still-pending children are `Abandoned` and the reservation released (once per process). Anchor sweep: once the confirmed commitment's next block is 16 deep and no child is pending, the anchors still unspent (`IBitcoinChainService.GetUnspentOutputAsync`, mempool included; a chain error skips the round and retries at the next block; without a chain service ours only when no child was ever made) are swept with empty signatures (`BuildAnchorSweep`, `nSequence` 16) to a wallet script when `AnchorCpfpPolicy.DecideAnchorSweep` says it pays for itself (output >= dust and fee <= `SweepMaxFeePerMille` of 330 x n: two anchors only below about 2.3 sat/vB, one never), else skipped (logged); published once per commitment and process through `IBlockchainMonitor.PublishTransactionAsync`, never stored (anyone may take them first).
- Package relay (NL-380, wave O7b): a commitment below bitcoind's mempool minimum is refused alone and its child as an orphan. When the monitor's `PublishAsync` refuses a new child, and every round in which the newest pending child was not replaced and bitcoind does not have it (`IBitcoinChainService.GetTransactionAsync`: `getrawtransaction` finds mempool transactions without txindex), the round sends the commitment row and that child row together through `IBitcoinChainService.SubmitPackageAsync` (Bitcoin Core 28+ 1p1c; after the lock, in `CompleteAsync`). The rows are the persisted `BroadcastTransactions`, so the first round after a restart sends the pair as a package again (the monitor keeps sending both one by one). A package refused for its fee (`PackageSubmitResult.IsFeeRefusal`) marks the child in memory, and the next round replaces it without waiting for `RbfIntervalBlocks` and even when, without a deadline, it pays the estimate (the estimate is below what the mempool takes); each replacement adds at least the BIP 125 increment, up to the cap. No `submitpackage` (older node) or no chain service: logged once, the pair is only sent one by one. Tests: the package cases of `AnchorCpfpServiceTests` (`FakeAnchorChain.Mempool`/`Packages`/`PackageAnswer`).
- The peer's commitment (NL-381, wave O7b; `AnchorCpfpService.Peer.cs`): a child belongs to the commitment its input 0 spends (a `LocalCommitment` row's txid: ours; anything else: the peer's), so no new purpose or column. Each round of a channel runs the peer's part first: found in bitcoind's mempool when the O8 `MempoolReactor` handed it over (`IAnchorCpfpService.OnPeerCommitmentInMempool(channel, tx, isNext)` for a `RemoteCommit`/`RemoteNextCommit` of an anchor channel; memory only; the channel then joins the rounds even while `Open`), or, for every round channel, by `getrawtransaction` of the peer's current and next commitment txids (rebuilt with the optional `ICommitmentOutputMapper`) and of the parents of our pending children of it (so after a restart, and when our own commitment is refused because the peer's holds the funding output while an HTLC deadline approached and the monitor failed the channel); it must spend the funding outpoint. A child only while it carries untrimmed HTLCs (the peer's dust limit): deadline = their earliest `cltv_expiry`, stake = our `to_remote` + those HTLCs; then the same `PlanChildAsync` as ours (first child, RBF every `RbfIntervalBlocks`, BIP 125 increment, cap), published one by one (no package: its parent is in the mempool). While it is there our own commitment gets no child and no package. The reservation is shared by both commitments' children (only one commitment can confirm): each part reports None/Active/Done and the inputs go back once neither is Active (a failed first child releases only when the other part has no pending child). End: a recorded close (`IOnchainResolutionDbRepository.GetCloseAsync`) of another transaction abandons the peer's children; the peer's confirmed commitment keeps its pending child with its inputs as ours does (anchor seen spent, or `ConfirmedCommitmentChildWaitBlocks`); a peer commitment bitcoind no longer has for `PeerCommitmentMissingBlocks` (6) blocks in a row (counted once per height, only with the monitor at bitcoind's tip, not halted) has its pending children abandoned. Sweep: a close of kind remote, remote-next, future or revoked, 16 blocks deep, read from the hand-over or from the block at `SpentAtHeight` (`GetBlockAsync`), both anchors swept like ours (same `PlanAnchorSweepAsync`, once per commitment and process). Tests: `Onchain/Anchors/AnchorPeerCpfpTests` (Bob's real signed commitment: script-valid child from the hand-over and from the probe, ours not bumped meanwhile, RBF, no child without an untrimmed HTLC, inputs kept until the anchor is spent, released after the child confirmed, abandoned when ours confirmed or the peer's left the mempool, anchors swept at +16) and `Onchain/Mempool/MempoolPeerCommitmentTests` (the hand-over, anchors only, never our own commitment).

- Provider: a `peer_storage` is kept only while we offer the feature and only from a peer with a channel in `IChannelMemoryRepository` (not Closed/Stale) or whose blob we already keep (`PeerStorageOptions.StoreWithoutChannel` lifts that); a blob from a peer without a channel yet (peers send theirs right after init, before our open_channel) is held in memory (latest per peer, at most `MaxPendingWithoutChannel` = 64 peers, forgotten after `PendingWithoutChannelLifetime` = 30 min) and adopted by the next round once a channel with that peer exists. The latest blob per peer is in memory (the table `PeerStorageBlobs` is loaded once, blocking, at the first connection or message) and written in its own scope at most once per `MinStoreInterval` (1 min; later ones wait for the round, every `BackupInterval`, or the disposal; the same bytes again write nothing); blobs are never deleted. `OnPeerInitialized` enqueues `peer_storage_retrieval` on the connection before it returns, and `PeerService` calls it before `WaitForInitAsync` completes, so it precedes `channel_reestablish`.
- Client: a peer that negotiated `option_provide_storage` gets our blob at its first connection of the process, when the round finds the blob's `Fingerprint` changed (a new channel, a closed one), and again when its `peer_storage_retrieval` holds another backup than the last we sent (by fingerprint, so a re-encryption of the same backup from before a restart is not re-sent; a retrieval handled before our init hook registered the connection forgets the last sent, so the next connection or round sends it). Retrievals are recorded only from peers with a channel, peers we sent a backup to, or when the blob is ours. A retrieval is read with `IPeerBackupBlobProvider.TryReadBlobAsync`; channels it names that `IChannelDbRepository.ExistsAsync` does not know are logged as possible data loss and kept in `GetRetrievals()` (`PeerBackupRetrieval.UnknownChannels`, this process only). NL-432: every recorded retrieval is also written, before any backup goes back to that peer, to `PeerStorageRetrievals` through `IUnitOfWork.PeerStorageRetrievalDbRepository` (one row per peer, the latest: raw blob, `ReceivedAt`, `MatchesLastSent`, the channel ids unknown at receipt; migration `AddPeerStorageRetrievals`); a failed write stays in memory and is retried by every round and by `StopAsync`. `ListRetrievalsAsync` reads the rows (plus unwritten ones, `Persisted = false`), decodes each blob again with the blob provider and checks every named channel with `ExistsAsync` now (`PeerBackupChannelStatus.UnknownWhenReceived`/`KnownNow`, `PeerStorageRetrievalReport.StillUnknown`); `ListStoredBlobsAsync` is the provider side (latest blob per peer, delayed writes included). Both back the daemon's `listpeerstorage` (ClientCommand 32). `IUnitOfWork.PeerStorageRetrievalDbRepository` has a throwing default for test doubles.

## Onion message transport and rate limit (BOLT 4 onion messages, wave M6 lane C: OM2-T1, OM2-T2)
- `PeerOutbox.TryEnqueueOnionMessage(OnionMessageMessage)`: onion messages are a separate, lower-priority class on their own queue. The send loop (one `SemaphoreSlim` released per write to either queue and once on close) takes the main FIFO first (channel messages, gossip, warnings, errors, the disconnect) and an onion message when the main queue is empty or, while gossip waits, in place of the gossip at the head after `PeerOutbox.GossipSendsPerOnionMessage` (8) gossip sends since the last onion message (a long gossip stream cannot starve them); a channel message, warning, error or disconnect at the head always goes first, so an onion message never delays one beyond the one send already on the wire; it goes out with `IPeerService.SendOnionMessageAsync` (a failure is logged at debug and the loop goes on). The queue is bounded by the constructor's `maxQueuedOnionMessages` (default `PeerOutbox.DefaultMaxQueuedOnionMessages` = 64, BOLT12 plan `OnionMessages:MaxOutboxPerPeer`; below 1 means 1): one more is refused, counted (`DroppedOnionMessageCount`) and reported through `onOnionMessageDropped`. `TryEnqueueDisconnect` drops the onion messages still waiting; `Complete` still sends them after the rest. `QueuedOnionMessageCount` is the waiting count.
- `PeerManager` implements the Domain port `IPeerOnionMessageOutbox` (`CanSendOnionMessage(nodeId)`, `TryEnqueueOnionMessage(nodeId, message)`): only the peer's current, not disconnected connection whose negotiated `Features.OptionOnionMessages` is not `No`; never connects (plan D6); false when the outbox is full or closed. `PeerManager.MaxOutboxOnionMessagesPerPeer` (public, default 64) is read when a connection's outbox is built: `AddApplicationServices` builds `PeerManager` through a factory that sets it from `OnionMessages:MaxOutboxPerPeer`. `QueuedOutboxOnionMessageCount` sums the current outboxes; every outbox's refusal increments the node-wide `DroppedOutboxOnionMessageCount` (the `dropped{reason=outbox_full}` source for M6-D's meter; the per-connection count is `PeerOutbox.DroppedOnionMessageCount`). When the peer's current session disconnects, `PeerManager` calls the optional `IOnionMessageRateLimiter.RemovePeer(nodeId)` (resolved lazily from the provider). `AddApplicationServices` registers `IPeerOnionMessageOutbox` as the `IPeerManager` singleton, like `IPeerGossipOutbox`.
- `OnionMessages/OnionMessageRateLimiter : IOnionMessageRateLimiter` (OM-R-01, MAY rate-limit by dropping): four token buckets, per peer and node-wide, in bytes and in messages; a message is admitted only if all four hold its tokens and then takes from all four (a drop takes nothing). `OnionMessageRateLimits` (same file) holds the limits; defaults per peer 64 KiB/s with a 256 KiB burst and 20 messages/s with a burst of 20, node-wide 640 KiB/s with a 2,560 KiB burst and 200 messages/s with a burst of 200; a rate or burst <= 0 turns that bucket off. Buckets start full and refill with `TimeProvider.GetTimestamp` (the constructor takes an optional `TimeProvider`, default `System`). A sweep every `SweepInterval` (256) calls forgets every peer whose buckets are full again, connected or not (a full bucket equals a fresh one), so memory stays bounded even when `RemovePeer` is never called or a late message re-adds a removed peer; `RemovePeer` forgets a full peer at once; a peer in debt is kept until refilled, so reconnecting never refills a peer early. `AdmittedCount`/`DroppedCount` for metrics. `AddOnionMessageServices` registers it (TryAdd) with the limits of `OnionMessageOptions` (`Peer*`/`Global*`) and the optional `TimeProvider`. The service (M6-D) calls `TryAdmit(peer, packetLength)` first in `HandleIncoming`.
- Tests: `Node/Services/PeerOutboxOnionMessageTests` (priority, gossip interleave, cap, disconnect, complete, failing send), `Node/Managers/PeerManagerOnionMessageTests` (port, negotiation gate, no connect, cap and node-wide drop count, `RemovePeer` on disconnect, none when a newer connection replaced the old one), `OnionMessages/OnionMessageRateLimiterTests` (manual clock: burst, refill, byte bucket, per-peer isolation, global caps, drops take nothing, disconnect/reconnect, sweep, concurrency).

## Static channel backups (SCB, wave rf1 lane R1 step 1; `Channels/Backup/`)
- Like LND's `exportchanbackup`/`verifychanbackup`. `ChannelBackupCodec` (plaintext v1, big-endian, one length-prefixed record per channel: channel id, peer node id and last known addresses, funding outpoint, capacity, funding height, SCID, flags (initiator, anchors, announced, inferred params), version, scid alias support, minimum depth, channel_type wire bytes, our key index with our funding key and payment basepoint (to check the derivation), the peer's funding key and four basepoints, both `ChannelParty`s; a reader skips unknown trailing record bytes and refuses anything else malformed) and `ChannelBackupCipher` (file = `"NLSCB"` + version 1 + 24-byte random nonce + XChaCha20-Poly1305 over the plaintext with the 6-byte header as AAD; key = HKDF-SHA256(node private key, salt `"NLightning static channel backup"`, info `"nltg-scb-v1"`)). Never private keys.
- `IChannelBackupService` → `ChannelBackupService` (singleton, `AddChannelBackupServices()`, not called by `AddApplicationServices`: the daemon's `AddChannelBackupNodeServices` does): `CreateSnapshotAsync`/`ExportAsync` read the database (`ChannelDbRepository.GetAllAsync` + the peer row); a channel is backed up (`IsBackedUp`) when its funding outpoint and the peer's key set are known and it is not None/Closed/Stale (OnchainResolving stays in: a restore still sweeps its `to_remote`). `VerifyAsync` decrypts with our key, checks chain and node id, and re-derives each channel's funding key and payment basepoint from its key index (`ILightningSigner.GetChannelBasepoints`); a bad backup is a result, not an exception. `WriteFileAsync` writes `ChannelBackupOptions.FilePath` atomically (`ChannelBackupFile`: owner-only temp file in the same directory, fsync, rename, then an fsync of the directory on Linux/macOS, best effort) only when the channels differ from the file (creation time ignored), never replaces a file holding channels while the database has no channel row at all (`KeptExisting`: a wiped database, the file is what the restore needs), moves a file that holds a channel the database has no row for at all (lost, e.g. a new channel opened on a wiped database before the restore) aside as `<file>.<time>.superseded` before writing the new one (rf1 review), and moves a file that does not decrypt with our key aside (`<file>.<time>.unreadable`); `ChannelBackupFile.MoveAside` never overwrites (`-2`, `-3`, ... on a name clash).
- `ChannelBackupMonitor` (singleton; the host calls `Start()`/`StopAsync()`): requests a write at start, on `IChannelMemoryRepository.OnChannelUpgraded`, on `OnChannelUpdated` only when a channel's backed-up flag or SCID changed (HTLC updates are filtered), and every `RefreshInterval` (10 min; catches channels that left memory without an event); requests coalesce, one write at a time after `WriteDelay` (1 s). Options `Node:Backup` (`FilePath`, `Enabled`, `WriteDelay`, `RefreshInterval`).
- Restore (step 2, like LND's `restorechanbackup`): `IChannelRestoreService` → `ChannelRestoreService` (singleton, `AddChannelBackupServices`; needs `IChannelManager`, `IPeerManager`, the chain monitor's `IOutpointWatcher`, `IMessageFactory`, `IMessageSerializer`, `ISha256`). `RestoreAsync(backup)`: decrypt, refuse another chain or node (`ChannelRestorePlanner.Validate`, `ChannelBackupException`), then the pure `ChannelRestorePlanner.Plan` per channel: `Restore`, `AlreadyExists` (any row with that id, even unreadable: never touched), `KeysMismatch` (the key index does not re-derive the recorded funding key and payment basepoint), `Duplicate`. Before anything is stored the key manager's channel index is advanced to at least the highest restored `KeyIndex` (`IChannelKeyIndexReserver` → `SecureKeyManagerKeyIndexReserver`: `GetNextChannelKey` until there, then an awaited key file write when the manager is the file-backed `SecureKeyManager`; `ISecureKeyManager` has no set-and-persist API, seam for the key-file owner), so a stale key file never gives a new channel a restored channel's keys. Per restored channel one save: the peer row (when missing: the backup's first address, else empty host/port 0), the recovery channel (`RecoveryChannels.Create`) with its serialized `error` (`RecoveryChannels.PeerErrorMessage`) and the `FundingOutput` watch; then `IChannelManager.RegisterExistingChannelAsync` and `TrackWatchedOutpoint`. Then `IFundingSpendLocator` (`ChainFundingSpendLocator` over `IBitcoinChainService`, TryAdd'ed; `NullFundingSpendLocator` without a chain service) checks the funding output: `gettxout` without the mempool, and when it is spent the blocks from the tip down to max(floor, tip - `Node:Backup:RestoreSpendSearchDepth` + 1) (4032) for its spend, `Node:Backup:RestoreSpendSearchBatchSize` (8) blocks read at once. The floor is the SCID height, else the backup's `FundingHeight` (a backup written before the funding confirmed; the search then also stops at the block holding the funding transaction, and none found means `NotConfirmed`); neither: `NotConfirmed` without reading the chain. A spend found is recorded on the funding watch (`MarkSpentAsync`, own save) and handed to `IOnchainChannelWatcher.HandleFundingSpentAsync` as the monitor would have (the peer force-closed on our wiped node's `error` before the restore). A spend older than the window (`FundingSpendLocation.HasOlderBlocksToSearch`, NL-430) is searched in the background by `IFundingSpendLocator.RescanAsync(entry, belowHeight)` down to the floor and handed over the same way once found; the channel's `Detail` starts with `FundingSpendRescan:`. Only a spend with nothing left to search (or no on-chain watcher) still reports `FundingAlreadySpent: ... rescan from height N`. The background search lives in memory: `IChannelRestoreService.ResumeSpendSearches()` (called by the daemon's `ChannelBackupHostedService` at every start; returns at once) looks the spend up again in the background for every recovery channel still waiting (`IsRecoveryChannel`, from `ChannelDbRepository.GetAllAsync`, entry rebuilt with `ChannelBackupService.CreateEntry`), each once the start-up registration has loaded it (the on-chain watcher ignores a channel that is not loaded; `ResumeRegistrationTimeout` 5 min, then skipped with a warning), so an interrupted search starts again (an unspent output costs one `gettxout`). A block the bitcoin node pruned stops the search with `FundingSpendStatus.BlocksPruned` (bitcoind's "pruned data" error; the blocks above it are searched first): the detail starts `FundingSpendPruned:` and says to sweep by hand, never to retry. The background search logs its progress every `ChainFundingSpendLocator.ProgressLogBlocks` (1,000) blocks and hashes the block transactions only for an entry without a short channel id (with one, the floor already is the funding block). Running `restorechanbackup` again re-checks every channel that is already a recovery channel still Failed (`AlreadyExists` with a "recovery channel waiting" detail): the spend lookup runs again and its peer is connected when it is not. Peers (NL-431): every known address is tried in order, graph `node_announcement` addresses first (`IGraphStore`, IPv4/IPv6/DNS via `ChannelBackupService.ConnectableAddresses`), then the peer row, then every backup address (the backup itself carries the peer row plus the announced addresses, at most `ChannelBackupService.MaxAddressesPerChannel` 8). The restore (which holds its lock) tries the first address and the next ones only within `ChannelRestoreService.ConnectBudget` (20 s); the addresses left are tried at once by the background loop, before its first backoff wait. A connected peer of a new recovery channel is disconnected first and waited for, up to `DisconnectTimeout` 10 s, so the new connection runs `OnPeerConnectedAsync` (still connected after it: `Connected` false with the reason). When no address answers, a background loop retries them (graph re-read each round) with `Node:ReconnectInitialDelay`/`ReconnectMaxDelay` backoff until the peer is connected, no recovery channel with it is still waiting (`IChannelMemoryRepository`), or the service is disposed. One restore at a time; the service is `IDisposable` (cancels the background work; `WaitForBackgroundWorkAsync` is internal for tests).

## Interactive-tx driver (splicing plan wave IT, IT4-T1/T2; `InteractiveTx/`, `Channels/Handlers/Tx*MessageHandler.cs`)
- `InteractiveTxDriver : IInteractiveTxDriver` (singleton, `AddInteractiveTxServices`) runs one negotiation per channel and keeps its completed attempts for RBF, in memory, touched only under the channel's lock. The BOLT 2 rules live in the Domain `InteractiveTxSession` (lane IT-A), reached through the `IInteractiveTxEngine`/`IInteractiveTxNegotiation` seam (`DomainInteractiveTxEngine` in production, the test `ReferenceInteractiveTxEngine` in `Application.Tests/InteractiveTx/TestDoubles/`).
- The dependent protocol (splice, dual-funded open, a test) implements `IInteractiveTxHost` and calls `StartAsync(InteractiveTxTerms, host)` under the lock: the host supplies the `SharedFundingSpec` (`GetSharedFundingAsync`), the commitment step (`CreateCommitmentSignedAsync`: our `commitment_signed` for the new funding, staged on the given unit of work), the shared input's signature/witness, the completion (`OnCompletedAsync` with the finalized transaction; broadcast is the host's) and `OnAbortedAsync`. When the host has verified the peer's `commitment_signed` it calls `OnCommitmentSignedReceivedAsync` (IT-SIG-03); our `tx_signatures` follow in the IT-SIG-01 order. `RequestRbfAsync` sends `tx_init_rbf` (floor `GetMinimumRbfFeeratePerKw`: max(25/24 x, +25 sat/kw)); a peer's `tx_init_rbf` is checked against the floor before `OnRbfRequestedAsync`.
- Persistence: the session row (`IUnitOfWork.InteractiveTxSessionDbRepository`, lane IT-C) is added in the save that precedes our `commitment_signed` (with the host's staged writes) and updated in one save per later transition (CS received, our/their `tx_signatures`, Signed, Aborted). `OnDisconnectedAsync` (called by `ChannelManager.OnPeerDisconnectedAsync` under each channel's lock for every channel `GetChannels(peer)` lists, wave qit integration) forgets an unstored negotiation (reservation released); a stored one stays for `ResumeAsync` (restart, `next_funding`).
- `tx_abort` rules: with no negotiation, 66-73 get our `tx_abort` and the peer's `tx_abort` is echoed; while our `tx_abort` waits for its echo, stale 66-73 and the echo itself are ignored, and `StartAsync`/`RequestRbfAsync` throw (a new attempt would take the echo as its own and the two drivers would bounce `tx_abort` forever); the wait ends with the echo, `OnDisconnectedAsync` or `InteractiveTxDriver.AbortEchoTimeout` (1 min, the driver's `TimeProvider`), and is not started when the peer already sent `tx_signatures` (it keeps the negotiation and never echoes). A `tx_abort` arriving after we echoed one, with no negotiation, RBF request or new quiescence (`IQuiescenceService.GetState` blocking updates) since, is not echoed again. After our `tx_signatures` we never send `tx_abort` (`AbortAsync` throws) and a peer's `tx_abort` is logged and ignored (the negotiation is kept, IT-ABT-01).
- Persist, then memory, then send: a failed save restores the attempt's negotiation and row in memory (the caller's message can be handled again); a fully signed attempt becomes completed (and the quiescence ends) only after its save. The driver checks IT-RBF-01 itself before the commitment step: an attempt that spends no input of an earlier completed one is aborted, whatever the engine does. `ResumeAsync(..., completedAttempts)` takes the channel's other stored rows (Signed ones become completed attempts; a Signed `model` is only remembered), `CreateTxSignaturesRetransmission(channelId, txid)` rebuilds our stored `tx_signatures` for `channel_reestablish` `next_funding` (the `commitment_signed` retransmission is the host's), and `GetChannels(peer)` lists the channels to pass to `OnDisconnectedAsync` (under each channel's lock) when the peer's connection closes. Wired at the wave qit integration: `AddApplicationServices` calls `AddInteractiveTxServices()` (and `AddInteractiveTxContributorServices()`), and `ChannelManager.OnPeerDisconnectedAsync` calls `OnDisconnectedAsync` for each of `GetChannels(peer)` under that channel's lock (c502fdb6). A peer message the engine rejects or throws on (`ArgumentException`/`InvalidOperationException`/...) is answered with `tx_abort`, never a warning or a channel failure. The wallet reservation is released on abort unless a completed attempt still holds it (an RBF that re-adds its inputs). Every end of a negotiation (abort sent/received, both signatures) calls `IQuiescenceService.Terminate` when one is registered.
- The nine handlers derive from `InteractiveTx/Handlers/InteractiveTxMessageHandler<T>`, resolve the driver per message (`GetService`), and answer `tx_abort` themselves when none is registered; `ChannelManager` dispatches 66-74 through `DispatchInteractiveTxMessageAsync` (unknown channel → `error`, as for every channel message). `open_channel2`/`accept_channel2` go to `Channels/DualFunding/` since wave DF (below).

## Adding an inbound channel message handler (the common task)
1. The message, payload and serializer must already exist. Check `src/NLightning.Domain/Protocol/Messages`, `src/NLightning.Infrastructure.Serialization/{Messages,Payloads,Tlv,Factories}` and `src/NLightning.Domain/Protocol/Constants/MessageTypes.cs`.
2. Create `Channels/Handlers/<Msg>MessageHandler.cs : IChannelMessageHandler<<Msg>Message>`. DI registers it automatically; don't add it by hand.
3. Add a `case MessageTypes.<X>:` to `ChannelManager.DispatchChannelMessageAsync`. It should call `ThrowIfUnknownChannelAsync` for a real channel id, then `GetChannelMessageHandler<T>(scope).HandleAsync(Cast<T>(message), ...)`. **If you skip this, the message falls into `default`, which throws a channel-scoped ChannelWarningException ("not supported yet"): the message is ignored, the peer gets a `warning` and stays connected.** That is the interim behavior for the dual-funding messages; we never fail the channel for a missing handler, since LND would force-close it. A message for a channel we don't know at all (memory, temporary channels of the peer, DB) gets an `error` for that unknown channel_id (BOLT 1 SHOULD; `ThrowIfUnknownChannelAsync`).
4. At the top of the handler, check the state and throw ChannelErrorException or ChannelWarningException (`NLightning.Domain.Exceptions`) with `(message, channelId?, peerMessage?)`. The peer receives `PeerMessage` when it is non-empty; otherwise the internal `message` is sent (`src/NLightning.Infrastructure/Node/Services/PeerCommunicationService.cs`), so always pass a `peerMessage` if the internal text shouldn't leak.
5. Persist through a scoped `IUnitOfWork` (`ChannelDbRepository` then `SaveChangesAsync`) and update `IChannelMemoryRepository`.
6. Build outbound messages only through `IMessageFactory`. Return the replies to the requester as a list, in wire order. To send to a peer other than the requester (e.g. when forwarding), raise an event that ChannelManager converts into `OnResponseMessageReady` (see `FundingConfirmedMessageHandler`), synchronously while the channel's lock is held, so wire order equals persist order. `ChannelManager` already holds the lock of the message's channel id around your handler: don't acquire it again (it is not reentrant), and never take a second channel's lock from inside a handler.
7. Add tests in `test/NLightning.Application.Tests/Channels/Handlers/` (xunit.v3 + Moq). Existing handler tests are named `HandleAsync_Condition_Outcome`; PeerManager tests use `Given_X_When_Y_Then_Z`.

## Gotchas
- Opening messages carry the **temporary** channel id. `currentState` only looks up real channels, so it is `None` for them, and the handlers must call `TryGetTemporaryChannelState(peer, id)` themselves. funding_created still carries the temp id. Only the initiator calls `UpgradeChannel`, which fires the `OnChannelUpgraded` event the Daemon's OpenChannelClientHandler waits on.
- `ForgetStaleChannels` only forgets fundee (non-initiator) channels still awaiting funding (`V1FundingSigned`/`ReadyForThem`) whose `FundingCreatedAtBlockHeight` is non-zero and at least 2016 blocks old. `FundingCreatedMessageHandler` sets that field to `IBlockchainMonitor.LastProcessedBlockHeight`; `HandleFundingConfirmationAsync` later overwrites it with the funding tx's first-seen height. Legacy rows with a zero height get it backfilled to the current block height on the next block, so their timeout starts then.
- `ConfirmUnconfirmedChannels` only re-drives `V1FundingSigned`/`ReadyForThem` channels whose watched funding tx is already `IsCompleted` (e.g. confirmed while offline). `HandleFundingConfirmationAsync` and `FundingConfirmedMessageHandler` ignore any other state, so channel_ready is sent once per confirmation. On reconnect, channel_ready is re-sent by the reestablish when both next_commitment_numbers are 1 (B2-RE-15).
- `AcceptChannel1MessageHandler` deliberately does not persist the initiator channel; `FundingSignedMessageHandler` saves it (V1FundingSigned, with its watches and the funding tx) before publishing the funding tx. On failure it removes the temporary channel by its temporary id (or the upgraded channel) and releases the locked UTXOs; failures in the validation checks before its `try` still rely on the Daemon's OpenChannelClientHandler to release them.
- Per-channel lock (NL-033): `ChannelManager` holds the lock for the message's channel id (the temporary id during the open flow, the real id after) around dispatch and the raising of the replies; block handlers (`ForgetStaleChannels`, the backfill, `HandleFundingConfirmationAsync` → `ConfirmFundingAsync`) take it per channel and re-check the channel's state under it. A channel is keyed by its temporary id until funding_created/funding_signed and by its real id afterwards; the per-peer inbound loop already serializes the open flow. The IPC open-channel operation takes it through `StartOpeningChannelAsync` (store the temporary channel + raise open_channel).
- scid aliases (NL-103): `FundingConfirmedMessageHandler` generates `LocalAliases` that avoid every real scid and alias in `IChannelMemoryRepository`, and reuses them if the channel already has some. `LocalAliases` is never persisted, so after a restart old aliases are forgotten: they are no longer recognized for incoming HTLCs and new ones are generated.
- Handler discovery uses reflection (`Assembly.GetTypes()`), which trimming or AOT may break.

## Payments (ABCD W1-B: ONION M4-T2/T3/T4/T6, invoices)
Registered by `Payments/PaymentsServiceCollectionExtensions.AddPaymentsServices()` (all singletons; called from `AddApplicationServices`). Returns one of `IncomingOnionForward` (scid, `amt_to_forward`, `outgoing_cltv_value`, `NextPacket`), `IncomingOnionFinal` (payload), `IncomingOnionMalformed` (BADONION code + `sha256_of_onion` → `update_fail_malformed_htlc`) or `IncomingOnionFailed` (`FailureMessage` + shared secret → `update_fail_htlc` with `IFailureOnionService.CreateErrorPacket`). Route blinding (M5, lane rf1-m5): blinded payloads are read only when `NodeOptions.Features.OptionRouteBlinding` is not `No` and an `IRouteBlindingService` is registered (`AddBitcoinInfrastructure` → `AddRouteBlindingServices`; `ReadsBlindedPayloads`); otherwise refused as before (an `update_add_htlc` `path_key` → malformed `invalid_onion_blinding`; a payload with `current_path_key` → `update_fail_htlc` `invalid_onion_blinding`, BOLT 2). When read, `ProcessAsync(..., incomingAmount, incomingCltvExpiry)` decrypts `encrypted_recipient_data` with the update_add path_key (reusing `PeeledOnion.PathKeySharedSecret`) or, at the introduction node, the payload's `current_path_key` (curve-checked, NL-077), checks it with `BlindedRecipientDataValidator` and returns `IncomingOnionForward`/`IncomingOnionFinal` with an `IncomingBlindedHop` (`IsIntroduction`, `RecipientData`, `NextPathKey`, and for a forward the `payment_relay` amount/expiry; null when the amounts were not passed, i.e. secret recovery). An introduction node's `invalid_onion_blinding` in `update_fail_htlc` waits a random 0..`HtlcSwitchOptions.BlindedErrorMaxDelay` (`Node:Switch:BlindedErrorMaxDelay`, 250 ms, zero turns it off; real time, never the test clock; BOLT 2/4 SHOULD). ## attribution_data (BOLT 4 attributable failures and hold times; ABCD W7-A, NL-326, NL-022, NL-325)
- Receive: `UpdateFailHtlcMessageHandler`/`UpdateFulfillHtlcMessageHandler` pass the TLV 1 `attribution_data` (and TLV 3 `fulfillment_payload`) to the engine, which keeps them on the persisted `HtlcRemoval`; `OutgoingHtlcFulfilled` carries them, `OutgoingHtlcFailed.Removal` too. ## HTLC switch (ABCD W2-B: ONION M4-T2 wiring, M4-T4, M4-T5, M4-T7; BOLT2 N8-T2)
`Payments/Switch/HtlcSwitch : IHtlcSwitch` (singleton), registered by `HtlcSwitchServiceCollectionExtensions.AddHtlcSwitchServices()` (called by `AddApplicationServices` right after `AddPaymentsServices()`, so production decorates the reestablish-gated probe), which `services.Replace`s the `LocalOnlyHtlcSwitch` and decorates the registered `IPeerLivenessProbe` with `LinkUpReplayingPeerLivenessProbe` (call it after `AddChannelOperationsServices()`/`AddPaymentsServices()` and any other probe registration; it throws when no probe is registered; idempotent). It works outside every channel lock and changes channels only through `IChannelOperations`; locks are taken in the order incoming HTLC (`KeyedAsyncLock<(ChannelId, ulong)>`) → payment hash (`KeyedAsyncLock<Hash>`) → channel (only inside `IChannelOperations` or the prune), never two channel locks. A `CommitmentRefusedException`/`KeyNotFoundException` (peer away, channel not loaded) is logged: nothing was persisted and the HTLC is still pending. The replays: `ChannelManager` at startup (every link is down then, so it resolves nothing that needs the peer) and `LinkUpEventReplayer`, which the decorated probe schedules on every `MarkLinkUp` (on the thread pool, without the caller's context: the snapshot and the unpruned settled rows are read under the channel lock, then `DerivePending` goes to the switch outside it; `WhenIdleAsync` for tests). A link is marked when a channel opens and after each channel_reestablish (N7, `ChannelManager.CompleteReestablishAsync`), which queues the channel's pending events itself; the decorated probe therefore skips the replay when the `ReestablishTracker` says the channel is `Reestablished`, so each link-up hands the events to the switch once (NL-264; without a registered tracker every `MarkLinkUp` replays).
- `IncomingHtlcLockedIn`: skipped unless the HTLC is still `RcvdAddAckRevocation` in memory. An HTLC with a `ForwardCircuitModel` is resumed, not peeled again: `Pending` → the channel HTLC that carries `HtlcOrigin.Forwarded(in)` (`FindHtlcsByOriginAsync`) is recorded (`Offered`), or, when there is none (the offer never persisted), the circuit is failed and the upstream HTLC gets `temporary_channel_failure`; `Failed` without an outgoing HTLC (refused offer) → fail upstream again; any circuit with an outgoing HTLC → the outgoing record (live, else its archived row from `LoadAsync`) resolves the upstream HTLC (preimage → fulfill, final failure → wrapped fail; then the archived row is pruned when the forward is done), otherwise it waits. Otherwise `IncomingOnionProcessor.ProcessAsync` runs with `replayOwner: null` (no replay check) when the HTLC's shared secret is already stored, else with `OnionReplayOwner(channelId, htlcId, cltvExpiry)` (processed before a restart or reestablish), the secret is stored (`RecordOnionSecretAsync`), then: malformed → `FailMalformedHtlcAsync`; failed → `FailHtlcAsync(CreateErrorPacket)`; final → receive; forward → forward.
- Receive (NL-253; `basic_mpp`, ABCD W6-B): under the payment-hash lock, an HTLC that was resolved while waiting for the lock (a set's fulfill or timeout acts on other parts under the hash lock, not their incoming locks) is skipped; then re-read the invoice and `FinalHopProcessor.Evaluate(..., acceptMultiPart)`; `InvoiceAlreadySettled` → fulfill at once (even at height 0); otherwise height 0 → `temporary_node_failure` (`acceptMultiPart` = `NodeOptions.Features.BasicMpp != No`, the default Optional). Every accepted HTLC joins the payment's in-memory `HtlcSet` (`Switch/HtlcSet.cs`, by payment hash; parts no longer waiting are pruned first: not `RcvdAddAckRevocation`, or on a channel closing on chain at or past its `cltv_expiry`; a single-part payment is a set of one). A part whose `total_msat` differs from the set's fails the **whole** set with PERM|15 (BOLT 4 SHOULD). While the parts' `amt_to_forward` are below `total_msat` the set is held and a `TimeProvider` timer runs (`HtlcSwitchOptions.MppTimeout`, default 60 s from the first part, BOLT 4 SHOULD ≥ 60 s); when it fires (background round, `WhenIdleAsync` for tests) an incomplete set is failed with `mpp_timeout` (0x17); a part whose failure is refused (peer away) is remembered and failed with `mpp_timeout` on its replay. A complete set is **committed** then fulfilled (`FulfillSetAsync`, ABCD W7-B, NL-322/NL-323): first the preimage is persisted on the record of every part but one (`MarkPartAsync`: under that channel's lock, one `IChannelStateDbRepository.ApplyAsync` of the record with `KnownPreimage` over `ChannelCommitments.Restore`, one save, then `ChannelModel.UpdateCommitments`; the switch's only direct snapshot write besides the prune), then the remaining part settles the invoice in its own save: its `FulfillHtlcAsync(..., stageWithFulfill)`, or, when its channel is closing on chain or that fulfill is refused (peer away, `KeyNotFoundException`), its mark (`MarkPartAsync` with the settle staged); the staging re-reads the invoice, checks it is still `Open` (else `InvoiceNotOpenException`: nothing settled, the marks are taken back and the set fails with PERM|15) and stages `Accept(sum of the held parts' amount_msat)` + `Settle` + `UpdateAsync`. So `Settled` means "every part of the set is fulfilled or carries the preimage". The other parts are then fulfilled; a refused one stays locked in with its mark, and its replay (link-up, startup) is a committed member (`InvoiceAlreadySettled`) and is fulfilled, so the whole set is always fulfilled (BOLT 4 MUST). An HTLC for a Settled invoice without the mark is failed with PERM|15 (NL-323). The invoice's `Settled` save is the commit point: the switch counts a mark as a committed member only when the invoice is `Settled` with that preimage, and so do the resolvers. A set that is no longer complete before the settle (a part resolved elsewhere, or its channel unloaded) has its marks taken back and is held again; any other failure before the settle (e.g. the settle's save) takes the marks back and holds the set with its timer, and a timer that finds its set complete fulfills it again (with the invoice's preimage). `FailPartAsync` (total_msat mismatch, `mpp_timeout`, canceled invoice) takes a leftover mark back before it fails a part or leaves it to time out on chain. The set itself lives in memory and is rebuilt from the persisted incoming HTLCs by the startup and link-up replays after a restart (its timeout starts again). `AddHtlcSwitchServices` registers `HtlcSwitch` as its own singleton and `IHtlcSwitch` as a factory over it, so the container disposes it (timers stopped, timeout rounds canceled; `IDisposable` + `IAsyncDisposable`) also behind `DustExposureHtlcSwitch`. Without `basic_mpp` a second HTLC for a settled hash fails with PERM|15; the switch never writes `Accepted` on its own.
- Channels closing on chain (ABCD W7-B, NL-316; `IsOnchain` = the incoming channel is `Failed` or `OnchainResolving`): the lock-in event comes from the BOLT 5 resolvers (below, `Onchain/Resolvers/FinalHopClaims`) every round while the HTLC pays an `Open` invoice of ours or a hash with no invoice (a keysend the switch has not accepted yet), or from a replay. Only a final-hop onion is acted on: a forward is never forwarded and nothing is failed (the channel cannot carry an update; the HTLC times out on chain), a malformed/failed onion, a rejected `FinalHopProcessor` decision, height 0 and an `mpp_timeout` only log. An accepted part joins its set like any other (it counts until the tip reaches its `cltv_expiry`); when the set completes, its "fulfill" is the mark (with the settle when it is the settling part), from which the resolvers claim it on chain (`HtlcRecord.KnownPreimage` of an incoming HTLC means "accepted as our final hop", B5-LCL-RO-02). `FailPartAsync` never fails an on-chain part (it only takes its mark back). Known limits: a part of a set committed before this change (no mark) is failed as a duplicate on its replay (regtest-only HTLCs so far, accepted under the schema-reset policy; no reconciliation of older data); (`HtlcExpiryMonitor` reads "preimage known" for a final-hop HTLC from the record's mark and the Settled invoice since NL-337); the invoice expiry is checked at the on-chain decision, so an HTLC locked in just before its invoice expired and never fulfilled is not claimed.
- Forward: the onion's scid resolves to an `Open` channel by `LocalAliases`, `RemoteAlias`, or the real scid unless `UseScidAlias` is `Compulsory` (option_scid_alias in the channel type, BOLT 2; a public channel with the feature only negotiated has `Optional` and is announced by its real scid, NL-348; `ThreeNodeSwitchTests.Given_ScidAlias*` on `ThreeNodeHarness.CreateAsync(bobCarolScidAlias: ...)`); `IForwardingPolicy` gets an `OutgoingChannelInfo` (usable = open, snapshot, no data loss, link up; `AvailableToSend` = settled local balance − our pending offered HTLCs − the reserve the peer requires). Then the circuit is saved `Pending`, `OfferHtlcAsync(..., HtlcOrigin.Forwarded(in))` (origin persisted with the add), and the circuit marked `Offered`; when the offer throws (a refusal, or anything else except a cancellation) the channel state decides: an HTLC carrying the origin → the circuit is marked `Offered`; none → the circuit is `Failed` and the upstream HTLC gets `temporary_channel_failure` with the update (a cancellation leaves it `Pending` for the replay).
- `OutgoingHtlcFulfilled` (origin `Forwarded`): fulfill upstream at once, then mark the circuit `Fulfilled` (also when the upstream fulfill was refused: the next replay of the upstream lock-in fulfills from the kept outgoing record). `OutgoingHtlcFailed` (only raised when irrevocable): `WrapErrorPacket` with the incoming shared secret (circuit, stored secret, or a re-peel), or `CreateErrorPacketFromMalformed` for `update_fail_malformed_htlc` (a bad hash length falls back to `temporary_channel_failure`), then `FailHtlcAsync` upstream and the circuit `Failed`. ## Onion-routing (BOLT 4) hooks here
The onion library (M1+M2+M3) is used by `LocalOnlyHtlcSwitch` (peel + error onion, see above) and by `Payments/` (above: payload parsing, replay set, final hop, forwarding policy); wiring the payment core into the switch comes with ABCD W2-B. All of these are DI singletons:
- `ISphinxService` (`src/NLightning.Domain/Protocol/Onion/Interfaces/`, registered by `AddBitcoinInfrastructure`).
- `IHopPayloadSerializer` (`src/NLightning.Domain/Serialization/Interfaces/`, registered by `AddSerializationInfrastructureServices`).
- `IOnionReplayStore` (registered by `AddInfrastructureServices` as the persistent `PersistentOnionReplayStore`, NL-078; `IOnionReplayCache` was removed).
- `HopPayloadValidator` is a static Domain class.

`IHopPayloadSerializer` is declared in Domain (`src/NLightning.Domain/Serialization/Interfaces/`), so Application uses it without referencing Serialization.

The receive and send paths below are implemented by `IncomingOnionProcessor` and `PaymentOnionFactory`; they are kept as the reference call order.

Receive path (M4-T2, after the HTLC is irrevocably committed):
1. `var packet = new OnionPacket(add.Payload.OnionRoutingPacket.Span);`
2. `var peeled = sphinx.PeelAsLocalNode(packet, paymentHash, blindedPathTlv?.PathKey);`. Pass the update_add_htlc path_key only, never the payload's current_path_key. It throws `OnionException`:
   - A BADONION code (`ex.FailureCode.IsBadOnion()`) → `update_fail_malformed_htlc` with `ex.FailureData` (sha256_of_onion).
   - `InvalidOnionPayload` → `update_fail_htlc` encrypted with `ex.SharedSecret` (M3).
   - With a path_key, every failure comes back as `InvalidOnionBlinding`.
3. `if (!replayCache.TryAdd(packet.Hmac.Span))`: this is a replay, so fail it. Record the HMAC only after the peel succeeded.
4. `var payload = await hopPayloadSerializer.DeserializeAsync(peeled.Payload);`. Its offsets already count the stripped length prefix.
5. `HopPayloadValidator.Validate(payload, peeled.IsFinal, hasUpdateAddPathKey)`. When update_add_htlc carried a path_key, remap any `OnionException` from here on to `invalid_onion_blinding`.
6. If `peeled.IsFinal`, go to final-hop processing. Otherwise forward `peeled.NextPacket` (`.ToBytes()`) on the channel for `payload.ShortChannelId`.

Keep `peeled.SharedSecret` with the HTLC: failures need it later.

Send path (M4-T6):
- Serialize each `HopPayload` with `IHopPayloadSerializer.SerializeAsync`. That writes no length prefix, and `OnionHop` expects none.
- Call `sphinx.ConstructWithSharedSecrets(hops, sessionKey, paymentHash)` with a fresh CSPRNG session key.
- Keep the per-hop secrets for error decryption (M3).

Other hooks:
- The ChannelManager cases and handlers for UpdateAddHtlc, UpdateFulfillHtlc, UpdateFailHtlc, UpdateFailMalformedHtlc, CommitmentSigned, RevokeAndAck and UpdateFee exist (N6-T1); the onion is peeled only after `IncomingHtlcLockedIn` reaches the switch.
- Forwarding: resolve the next hop by ShortChannelId or its aliases through `IChannelMemoryRepository.FindChannels`. Then call `MessageFactory.CreateUpdateAddHtlcMessage`, which can't attach a BlindedPathTlv yet. Send the result through the `OnResponseMessageReady` event path.
- Failures: `CreateUpdateFailHtlcMessage` expects an already obfuscated `reason`, and no error-onion code exists yet (M3). `CreateUpdateFailMalformedHtlcMessage` takes sha256_of_onion plus a raw ushort failure code; cast from `FailureCode`.

## BOLT 5 remote-commitment resolution (plan O4, ABCD W5-C; `Onchain/Resolvers/`)
- `RemoteCommitResolver : IOutputResolver` (the shared port `Domain/Onchain/Interfaces/IOutputResolver.cs`; a stateless singleton registered by `Remote/RemoteCommitResolutionServiceCollectionExtensions.AddRemoteCommitResolutionServices()` together with `IRemoteSweepDestination` → `WalletRemoteSweepDestination` (an unused P2WPKH wallet address from a scope of its own, handed to the monitor), `IRemoteCommitmentSource` → `ChainRemoteCommitmentSource` (the commitment read from the block at `ChannelCloseModel.SpentAtHeight`, only for data loss or a rebuild whose txid differs) and `RemoteResolutionOptions` (`ReasonableDepth` 6, `IrrevocableDepth` 100); called from `AddApplicationServices` after `AddOnchainServices()`, options bound from `Node:Onchain` by the daemon) handles `ChannelCloseKind.RemoteCommitment`, `RemoteNextCommitment` (the snapshot's `RemoteNextCommit`, B5-RMT-01), `FutureCommitment` (data loss, B5-RMT-03) and, since NL-320, `Unknown` (a funding spend that is no known commitment, handled exactly like data loss with a B5-GEN-06 alert). It reads the channel, HTLC origins, circuits and payments through its own scope, never saves, never publishes and keeps no state: `ResolveAsync(close, outputs, height)` and `OnOutputSpentAsync(close, output, spender, height)` return `OutputResolverAction`s (row upserts, `ResolutionOutput` watches, `BroadcastAction`s, `StageWriteAction`s, `RaiseChannelEventAction`s, `AlertAction`s) that the executor applies in one save. The resolver moves rows only between Pending, Waiting, Broadcast and Ignored; the executor marks a spent output Resolved and, 100 blocks on, Irrevocable. Alerts come from the spending witness (at spend time) and from the first data-loss round only.
- Final-hop decision (both resolvers, ABCD W7-B, NL-316/NL-322; `Resolvers/FinalHopClaims.GetFinalHopDecisionAsync`): while an unspent received HTLC output (ours: `LocalReceivedHtlc`; theirs: `RemoteOfferedHtlc`) belongs to an HTLC that is `RcvdAddAckRevocation` without our removal, below its `cltv_expiry`, not forwarded (no origin row, no circuit) and whose hash is an `Open` invoice of ours or has no invoice (keysend: the switch makes the record when it accepts; lane lh1-l3 review), the round raises `IncomingHtlcLockedIn` for it (every round, the switch is idempotent): the switch decides as final hop and persists the preimage on the record, and the next round claims with it. The resolvers read `IInvoiceDbRepository` and `IForwardCircuitDbRepository` for it (test stores: `InMemoryOnchainStore.Invoices`, `LocalCommitResolutionHarness.Invoices`).
- Rules: `to_remote` swept at once (D5; also after data loss through `ICommitmentOutputMapper.FindPaymentToRemote`); our offered HTLCs claimed with `<sig> <>` at `nLockTime = cltv_expiry` once the tip reaches it; the peer's offered HTLCs claimed with `<sig> <preimage>` before `cltv_expiry`, only when irrevocably committed (state >= 34) and only with an allowed preimage: our own fulfill of that HTLC (`HtlcRecord.Removal`), the preimage the switch persisted on it when it accepted it as our final hop (its own `KnownPreimage`, above), or the preimage its forward learnt downstream (the outgoing record, found through `FindHtlcsByOriginAsync(Forwarded(in))` or the circuit, live or archived: `KnownPreimage` or its fulfill). The switch cannot fulfill upstream once the channel is closed, so that downstream record is where the preimage is; never an invoice preimage alone (B5-LCL-RO-02; a final-hop HTLC whose fulfill was refused before the close is claimed once the switch accepted it, above). One transaction per output (fee from `IFeeService` through `SweepFeePolicy.Decide`; an output that does not pay its own fee is `Ignored`, but an ignored HTLC of ours still gets its upstream fulfill (a later preimage spend) or fail (as an HTLC without output)). The planner (`OutputResolutionPlanner`) decides every step.
- Upstream: a preimage of our offered HTLC seen on chain is staged into the HTLC's record (`HtlcRecord.KnownPreimage`, through `IChannelStateDbRepository.ApplyAsync`, and into the loaded channel's snapshot) in the round that raises `OutgoingHtlcFulfilled`, so the switch's own replays (`DerivePending` at link-up/startup) derive the fulfill too; our confirmed timeout claim (or an HTLC without output, B5-RMT-LO-03: at once when no other valid commitment has an output for it, else at `ReasonableDepth`) raises `OutgoingHtlcFailed` with `RemoteHtlcSwitchEvents.OnchainTimeoutKind` (= `HtlcRemovalKind.OnchainTimeout`), or with the peer's own failure when it had sent one. Both are raised again **every round** until the upstream actually has its removal: the incoming HTLC (origin `Forwarded`) has a removal, is final or gone, or its channel is not Open and the circuit is resolved; the payment (origin `Local`) is no longer in flight; an HTLC without a stored origin is raised until its row is irrevocable (the switch only logs a removal it cannot send).
- Data loss: every output of the commitment gets a row and a watch; `to_remote` is swept, the others are `OutputDescriptorKind.Unknown`, and any spend of them is searched (`HtlcWitnessParser.TryExtractPreimage`) for the payment hashes of our still-open offered HTLCs of the snapshot (fulfilled as above). Unknown rows become Ignored only once the commitment is irrevocable and every such HTLC is resolved upstream or expired `IrrevocableDepth` blocks ago. A preimage revealed by a later spend is then a loss for a forward (the upstream was failed, as it would have been timed out on chain anyway). Our own payment (origin `Local`) has no upstream deadline: it is failed only at `cltv_expiry + IrrevocableDepth`, and its Unknown rows stay watched until then unless the payment succeeded (a failed payment does not release them), so a preimage the peer reveals meanwhile still makes it succeed.

## Interactive-tx wallet contributor (splicing plan IT2-T3, wave IT lane IT-B; `InteractiveTx/WalletInteractiveTxContributor.cs`)
- `WalletInteractiveTxContributor : IInteractiveTxContributor` (**singleton**, `InteractiveTxContributorServiceCollectionExtensions.AddInteractiveTxContributorServices()`, TryAdd, as itself and as the interface; the integrator calls it after `AddInteractiveTxBitcoinServices()`). It needs `IFeeInputSelector`, `ILightningSigner`, `IUtxoMemoryRepository`, the Domain ports `IWalletPrevTxSource`, `IPrevTxInspector` and `IInteractiveTxTransactionParser` (all from Infrastructure.Bitcoin's `AddInteractiveTxBitcoinServices`), and optionally `IServiceScopeFactory` (the stored negotiations, `IUnitOfWork.InteractiveTxSessionDbRepository`) and `IAnchorReserveService`. It no longer uses any Infrastructure.Bitcoin type. `ContributeAsync`: no wallet input without a positive `WalletAmount` (the request's outputs are returned alone: a splice-out paid from the channel balance, plan D16); otherwise one `IFeeInputSelector` reservation (purpose `itx:<purpose>:<channel id>`; mined P2WPKH/P2TR outputs only, so `require_confirmed_inputs` always holds, re-checked against the UTXO set when asked) for wallet amount + outputs, charged `ExtraWeight` + our outputs + our inputs each at least the minimum witness weight 107 (BOLT 3 Appendix G "Expected Fee Calculation"; a P2TR input weighs 231 but the peer charges 271; a short reservation is released and retried with more weight, 3 attempts) + a P2WPKH change, which is kept only at 294 sat or more (else fee). Each input's `prevtx` comes from `IWalletPrevTxSource` and must inspect to the same txid, amount and script; a `prevtx` longer than `MaxPrevTxLength` (65,483 = 65,535 - the other `tx_add_input` fields) cannot go on the wire (u16 `prevtx_len`), so its reservation is held while the selector picks again (at most 3 times) and released at the end; none left → `InvalidOperationException`. The anchors reserve is checked after reserving (held reservations released first), as for a withdraw. Any failure releases the reservation.
- `SignAsync`: the constructed transaction's bytes must be what its metadata says (parsed with `IInteractiveTxTransactionParser`: txid, version 2, locktime, each input's outpoint and sequence, each output's amount and script, in ascending `serial_id` order, no witness), and it must contain each of our inputs once, as ours, with our sequence, and each of our outputs (amount and script) as ours, else `InvalidOperationException` and nothing signed; then `SignWalletTransaction(tx, reservationId, otherSpentOutputs)` (only that reservation's inputs; P2TR needs the other spent outputs) and our witnesses in ascending `serial_id` order.
- IT-ABT-01, durable: with a session store, `SignAsync` refuses a reservation that no stored, unresolved, not `Aborted` negotiation holds (`LocalContribution.ReservationId`): the driver must store the negotiation (from our `commitment_signed`, BOLT 2) before it asks for our witnesses. `ReleaseAsync` refuses (logged) while such a stored negotiation holds the reservation, also after a restart, and while this process signed it; the driver marks the negotiation `Aborted` (saved) before releasing. `ConfirmAsync` ends it once the selector sees an input spent. Without a store (no scope factory, or a unit of work whose `InteractiveTxSessionDbRepository` throws `NotSupportedException`) only the in-process guard applies (warning logged at signing). Release, sign and confirm run one at a time (one gate), so a release never frees a reservation a concurrent signature is using. `ReleaseOrphanedReservationsAsync` (host, once at startup, before negotiations): releases every `itx:` reservation that no stored negotiation holds and this process did not hand out; without a store it releases nothing. Not done (ledger follow-up): a signed negotiation that can never confirm (the peer double-spent its input) keeps its reservation until an input is spent; there is no "abandon by double-spending our own inputs" path yet.
